**Tajweed** (تجويد) is the set of rules for reciting the Quran correctly: how each letter is pronounced, how long vowels are held, when sounds are nasal, and how to move from one word to the next.

The word comes from a root meaning to make something better or more precise. The goal is not melody, but a recitation that does not change the meaning of the text.

## Why it matters

The Quran calls for a measured recitation. Surah Al-Muzzammil, verse 4, asks the reciter to recite it with *tartīl*: slowly and clearly. Scholars turned this requirement into a discipline of its own.

## The main families of rules

- **Makhārij**: the points of articulation of letters, in the throat, the tongue and the lips.
- **Madd**: the lengthening of long vowels.
- **Ghunna**: a nasal sound on *nūn* and *mīm*.
- **Qalqalah**: a slight bounce on five letters when they carry no vowel.
- **Rules for nūn sākin and tanwīn**: *izhār*, *idghām*, *ikhfāʾ* and *iqlāb*.

## Where to start

Start with the letters: without accurate pronunciation, the advanced rules stay abstract. A qualified teacher remains the best reference, and a listening tool can help you spot mistakes between lessons.
